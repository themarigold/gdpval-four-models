I've built the Deep Dive Strategy Presentation as a 15-page widescreen PDF: a cover, an executive summary, five core slides, an appendix divider, six appendix pages and a Q&A page. Every figure in it needs checking before it goes to the CEO. I had no internet access, so the market data comes from my own knowledge of public sources up to May 2024 rather than a fresh pull from the Korea Transportation Safety Authority (KOTSA) or taxi association portals. All SuperK-T starting numbers are my assumptions, flagged as estimates on the slides: about 4% share, 0.8M monthly users, 12k weekly active drivers, 6k franchise vehicles, a 9-minute pickup time.

**The core recommendation:** spend KRW 170bn from H2 2024 to 2026, released in stages, to move SuperK-T from about 4% to 13% of app-booked taxi trips. Each stage is released only if the previous stage's targets are met.

**Core slides**
1. **Executive summary:** where we stand, three moves in sequence, the 2026 targets and the decisions requested.
2. **Market reality and strategic imperatives:**
   - The market is about 250k taxis and roughly KRW 11tn in fares a year.
   - Kakao T takes about 94% of app bookings, per the Fair Trade Commission's 2023 ruling.
   - Corporate-taxi drivers fell about 31% between 2019 and 2023, and Seoul's base fare rose 26% in 2023.
   - Kakao is under regulatory pressure: a KRW 27bn fine, a cut to a 2.8% fee and an open accounting review.
   - The slide gives a candid view of SuperK-T's weaknesses and five strategic priorities.
3. **Growth plan, part 1: drivers and vehicles ("fill the idle shift").**
   - Seoul's roughly 22k corporate taxis have only about 21k drivers, so around half of possible shifts go unused.
   - From 20 August 2024, the new monthly-wage rule raises operators' fixed costs. This makes them receptive to partnerships that fill those shifts.
   - Four levers: partnerships with taxi companies, a 0%-then-2.5% commission offer for drivers, a training academy for new drivers, and late-night "power zones".
4. **Growth plan, part 2: riders.** Four segments Kakao serves poorly (inbound tourists, business accounts, late-night riders aged 20–40, airport trips). It also sets out the Korean app basics we must match, such as local payment apps and building-entrance search.
5. **Future-proofing:** electric-vehicle financing and charging, readiness for robotaxi pilots, shared taxi rides (legal since 2022), links with public transport and AI dispatch. Spending on these is capped at about 9% of the budget.
6. **Roadmap, targets and investment:** three phases (Aug–Dec 2024, 2025, 2026), a target table, the budget split and five decisions for the CEO and Regional.

**Appendices:** market data charts, a regulation-by-regulation table with our response, a competitor comparison, a base/bear/bull financial scenario, risks with mitigations, and sources and method.

**Things to confirm before sharing:**
- **Monthly-wage rule:** the 20 August 2024 national rollout was still being debated in May 2024 and could be delayed. The plan is built to work either way.
- **Financial model:** it assumes about 45% of taxi trips are booked by app, an average SuperK-T fare of KRW 13,000, and a net take rate rising from 3% to 5.5%. Finance should validate all three before the investment decision.
