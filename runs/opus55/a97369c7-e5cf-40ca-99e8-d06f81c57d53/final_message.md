**1. Board authority and whether the agreement can be enforced**
- **Before the 2024 amendment:** Under *Moelis*, Marcus's veto right and his right to appoint directors and officers would likely have been invalid under DGCL § 141(a).
- **After the amendment:** Senate Bill 313 added new DGCL § 122(18), which applies to existing contracts like Marcus's January 2024 agreement and now generally allows rights like these. The key exception is that a provision can't be enforced to the extent it is "contrary to the certificate of incorporation."
- **Avalon's charter:** The charter says the business shall be "*exclusively* managed by the board." That gives a substantial argument that Marcus's rights are unenforceable, though no Delaware court has yet interpreted this exception.
- **Other points:**
  - The director-appointment right is the weakest of Marcus's rights, because directors are normally elected by stockholders.
  - The bylaws (§ 109) and § 142(b) also reserve officer selection to the board, which undercuts the officer-appointment right.
  - The memo notes it's unclear whether a commercial contract like Velridge's even counts as a "corporate transaction" requiring Marcus's consent.

**2. The board's fiduciary exposure**
- **No abdication:** Under *McMullin*, directors cannot simply defer to a powerful stockholder, whether or not the agreement is enforceable.
- **Facts that increase risk:**
  - The board's stated aim was to "appease" Marcus, which supports a bad-faith claim.
  - Every director was appointed by Marcus, which raises independence concerns.
  - Avalon's charter has no provision limiting directors' personal liability for damages.

**3. Marcus's fiduciary exposure**
- Marcus is likely a controlling stockholder despite his minority stake, given his contractual control and the board's actual deference (*Kahn v. Lynch*, *Voigt*, *Basho*).
- Under *Sears Hometown*, a controller who blocks the board's action to deliberately harm the company for personal reasons breaches the duty of loyalty. His veto would be hard to defend under any standard of review.
