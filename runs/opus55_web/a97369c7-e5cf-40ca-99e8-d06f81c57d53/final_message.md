**Bottom line on each question**

1. **Board authority and enforceability.** The Agreement would likely have been invalid under the Court of Chancery's *Moelis* reasoning. But new § 122(18) authorizes these rights 
"Notwithstanding § 141(a)"
, and Marcus's veto came after it took effect. The biggest open question is specific to Avalon: whether the charter's "exclusively managed" wording is a charter restriction that blocks § 122(18). The bill's synopsis says 
"a general recitation in the certificate of incorporation of the default provisions of § 141(a) would not be sufficient to render inoperable the provisions of § 122(18)"
. The memo concludes that view more likely wins, but treats the point as untested. It also explains why Marcus's appointment right probably works only as a promise by Avalon, not as a right to seat directors without a stockholder vote.
2. **The board's fiduciary duties.** The synopsis says § 122(18) doesn't relieve directors or stockholders of their duties, including 
"with respect to deciding whether to perform, or cause the corporation to perform, or to breach, the contract"
. The memo weighs a good-faith contract-compliance defense against three risks:
   - a *McMullin*-style argument that the board abdicated its judgment to Marcus;
   - the board's stated aim of "appeasing" Marcus, which suggests it lacked independence;
   - Avalon's lack of a charter provision shielding directors from damages.
3. **Marcus's fiduciary duties.** Because he can appoint the board, he is likely a controlling stockholder under *Kahn* and the new statutory definition in § 144(e)(2). The memo walks through the competing standards from *Sears Hometown*, *Basho* and *Voigt*. It notes that the new statutory protection for controllers does not cover 
breach of the duty of loyalty, acts or omissions not in good faith or which involve intentional misconduct or a knowing violation of law
, which is where a veto driven purely by personal animus would fall.

**Developments after 2024 that the memo includes**
- **Supreme Court reversal of *Moelis* (Jan. 20, 2026).** The Delaware Supreme Court 
reversed the Court of Chancery on procedural grounds without reaching the merits of whether the challenged provisions violated Section 141(a)
. It held that such provisions are voidable, not void, and so subject to laches. That matters for timing: a facial challenge to Avalon's January 2024 Agreement may be time-barred if not brought within roughly three years of signing.
- **SB 21 upheld.** On February 27, 2026, 
in *Rutledge v. Clearway Energy Group*, the Delaware Supreme Court upheld the constitutionality of Sections 1 and 3 of Senate Bill 21
. That supports applying the new controller definition and protections to the 2024 veto.
