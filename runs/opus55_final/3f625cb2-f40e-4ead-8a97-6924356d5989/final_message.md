**The memo's main conclusions:**
- **COPPA:** Collecting a child under 13's name and home address without a parent's verified consent is exactly what COPPA forbids. But parents can't sue under COPPA themselves; only the FTC and state attorneys general can enforce it.
- **California law:** The client's real claims for his son are under California law: the state constitution's right to privacy, the privacy tort of intrusion upon seclusion, and the Unfair Competition Law. The memo also covers the California Consumer Privacy Act: the parent must opt in before a child's data is sold or shared, but individuals can only sue under it for data breaches.
- **Key open question:** YouTube's likely defense is that it didn't know his son's age, for example if the boy signed up with a false adult birth date. The memo lists the facts that would help or hurt.

**Cases summarized:**
- *FTC & New York v. Google/YouTube* (2019): YouTube paid $170 million to settle children's-data charges and remains under a court order.
- *Jones v. Google* (9th Cir. 2023): COPPA doesn't block state-law privacy claims. This is the key case for the client.
- *In re Nickelodeon*, *In re Facebook Internet Tracking*, *Hill v. NCAA*, *Hernandez v. Hillsides*, and *Kwikset* (the money-loss requirement for UCL claims).
