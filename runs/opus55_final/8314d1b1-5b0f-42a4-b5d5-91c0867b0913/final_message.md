**The memo's main conclusions:**
- **Elias's position:** he is a controlling stockholder of Clarivon Group, the buyer, and also owns all of Luminor Dynamics, the seller. That puts the deal in the category Delaware courts scrutinize most closely.
- **Default standard:** without protective measures, a court would apply "entire fairness," and Elias would have to prove both fair process and fair price. The *Southern Peru* case, where damages exceeded $2 billion, is the closest real-world example to this deal.
- **Pre-2025 path to lighter review:** before the 2025 amendments, the court would defer to the board's judgment only if the deal used both an independent special committee and a majority-of-the-minority vote from the outset (the *MFW* and *Match* cases).
- **March 2025 amendments (SB 21):** for a deal like this one, which is not a going-private transaction, either committee approval or a vote of the disinterested stockholders is enough to protect it from fiduciary-duty claims. The "from the outset" requirement is gone, and exchange-independent directors are presumed disinterested.
- **Recommendation:** use both protections from the start anyway. The statute's elements can still be litigated, its constitutionality has been challenged, and using both also satisfies the older case-law standard. The memo lists nine concrete steps for Elias.
