**Bottom line in the memo:** YouTube's collection of the son's name, gender, age, and address without parental consent likely violates COPPA, and likely California privacy law too. The key fact is whether YouTube knew, or had reason to know, that the child was under 13. COPPA gives parents no private right to sue, so any lawsuit would rely on California claims.

**What the memo covers:**
- **Violations:** COPPA (verifiable parental consent, parental review and deletion rights), the CCPA's under-13 consent rule, the California constitutional privacy right, the UCL, and CalOPPA. It also explains why the Age-Appropriate Design Code and the wiretap statute are weak or inapplicable here. It lists YouTube's likely defenses.
- **Case law:** the 2019 FTC/New York AG settlement with Google and YouTube over children's data, plus Ninth Circuit and California Supreme Court authority on COPPA preemption, intrusion, and constitutional privacy.
- **Options:** a data request, regulatory complaints, a demand letter, a lawsuit, and a class action, each with pros and cons. The memo also flags filing deadlines and notes that the child's own claims are tolled until he turns 18 but the parent's claims are not.
- **Next steps:** preserve evidence first, do not delete the account yet, answer specific fact questions, and decide on litigation within 30 days.
